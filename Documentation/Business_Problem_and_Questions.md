# Business Problem & Business Questions

## 1. Real-World Business Problem

Organizations such as IT companies, hospitals, and other employers may need a structured way to understand how sleep patterns and lifestyle indicators vary across their workforce or target population. Stress, work schedules, bedtime screen exposure, caffeine and alcohol habits, and physical activity can be examined alongside sleep duration, sleep quality, and perceived rest.

Without analysis, decision-makers may find it difficult to identify groups with concerning patterns, determine which indicators deserve further investigation, and plan relevant wellness education or support.

**Business problem statement:** How can organizations use sleep-health and lifestyle data to identify sleep-related risk patterns, understand differences across occupational and demographic groups, and make better-informed decisions about wellness initiatives?

## 2. Real-World Problems and Analytical Responses

| # | Problem to investigate | Proposed analytical response |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Some groups may report shorter sleep duration. | Compare average sleep hours across occupations, age groups, and work patterns. |
| 2 | Higher stress may occur alongside lower sleep quality. | Compare stress and sleep-quality scores by group and risk category. |
| 3 | Bedtime screen exposure may differ across sleep outcomes. | Create screen-time bands and compare average sleep duration and quality. |
| 4 | Caffeine or alcohol habits may vary across groups. | Compare sleep outcomes across consumption categories. |
| 5 | Stress may differ by occupation. | Rank occupations by average stress score and inspect group sizes. |
| 6 | Cognitive performance may vary across sleep-risk groups. | Compare average cognitive performance by risk category. |
| 7 | Shift work and chronotype may relate to sleep patterns. | Segment records by shift-work status and chronotype. |
| 8 | Wellness support may need to be targeted. | Use dashboard filters to locate groups with less favorable indicators. |
| 9 | Decision-makers may lack repeatable wellness metrics. | Define consistent KPIs for sleep duration, quality, stress, and recorded risk. |
| 10 | Multiple variables are difficult to interpret separately. | Combine metrics and charts in an interactive dashboard. |

## 4. Problem-to-Solution Flow

- **Problem:** A particular occupational group may show high average stress and lower average sleep duration.
- **Analysis:** Compare average stress, sleep duration, sleep quality, risk-category share, and record counts by occupation.
- **Proposed response:** Investigate work schedules and consider appropriate wellness education or employee-support resources.
- **Evaluation:** If new real-world data becomes available, monitor the same KPIs over time and assess whether outcomes change.

## 5. Business Objectives

1. Identify occupational and demographic groups with concerning sleep patterns.
2. Explore the association between stress, sleep duration, sleep quality, and lifestyle indicators.
3. Compare cognitive performance across recorded sleep-risk categories.
4. Build an interactive dashboard for exploring sleep-health indicators.
5. Communicate data-supported recommendations for wellness planning.

## 6. Interpretation and Limitations

The dataset supports descriptive and exploratory analysis. Associations do not demonstrate causation. A risk category should not be treated as a medical diagnosis unless validated clinical definitions and evidence are available. Confirm the dataset's representativeness and synthetic/real-world status against the source documentation before drawing external conclusions.
